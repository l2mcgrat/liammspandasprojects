Law & Policy / Public Policy Study 02 · Referendum Anatomy

Three ballots
in one.

On October 19, 2026, Albertans cast 2,070,634 ballots across 10 questions — passing all 5 immigration & voter-ID proposals, rejecting all 4 constitutional amendments, and voting 74.95% to 25.05% to remain in Canada.

01 / The 10-question split

Voters said yes to provincial immigration & ID rules, no to constitutional amendments, and 3-to-1 to stay in Canada.

The October 19, 2026 ballot was not a single referendum: it bundled three distinct propositions, and the electorate split them surgically. Every non-constitutional immigration and voter-ID question passed (52.3%–59.8%); every constitutional amendment failed just below 50% (46.8%–49.8%); and Question 10 rejected commencing a binding separation referendum by nearly fifty points (74.95% to 25.05%).

02 / How each demographic voted

Region, party coalition, age, turnout, and early mail ballots.

Remain won every region of the province — including Rural Alberta (57% Remain vs. 35% Option 2) — while Edmonton (77%) and Calgary (75%) anchored the 3-to-1 margin. The sharpest fracture ran straight through the governing United Conservative Party: UCP supporters split 52% Option 2 to 44% Remain, whereas NDP voters backed Remain 93% to 4%.

03 / Inside the two coalitions

Why a 25% Option 2 vote is ~18% actual separatism — and why Option 2 voters were the most optimistic.

Abacus Data and Research Co. exit polling show that neither camp was monolithic:

  • Leverage vs. Exit inside Option 2: Only 32% of Option 2 voters named independence as their main reason; 23% wanted a better fiscal deal inside Canada, 22% believed Albertans deserved a direct vote, and 19% wanted to send Ottawa a message. Asked directly about an independent Alberta, support sits at ~18% province-wide.
  • The 47-point Canadian identity gap: 76% of Remain voters feel strongly attached to Canada (and 51% to Alberta); among Option 2 voters, only 21% feel strongly attached to Canada versus 68% to Alberta. Still, 65% of all referendum voters call themselves “Canadians first, Albertans second” (versus 22% “Albertans first”).
  • The age & optimism twist: Voters 60+ turned out at 85% (vs. 38% under 30) and held equal 71% attachment to both Canada and Alberta. Meanwhile, 18–34 voters were most open to US statehood (21% vs. 6% among 55+), and Option 2 voters were more likely than Remain voters to say Alberta's best days are ahead (59% vs. 44%).
