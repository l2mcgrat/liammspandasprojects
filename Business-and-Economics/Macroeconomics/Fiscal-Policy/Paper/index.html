1 Executive Summary & Generational Accounting Identity

To evaluate inter-generational fiscal equity without inflation distortion or cohort-size bias (such as the post-war Baby Boom bulge versus Generation X), we construct a population-normalized Generational Accounting panel in constant 2024 Canadian dollars (\(\text{2024 CAD}\)) spanning 1985 to 2025. Integrating Statistics Canada’s Distributions of Household Economic Accounts (DHEA), Survey of Household Spending (SHS), Census/LFS Commuting Supplements, Canadian Tobacco and Nicotine Survey (CTNS), and CIHI National Health Expenditure age-cost curves, we define the real per-capita Net Fiscal Incidence (\(\text{NFI}_{g,t}\)) and Fiscal Reward-to-Tax Ratio (\(\Lambda_{g,t}\)) for generation \(g\) in period \(t\):

\[\begin{equation} \text{NFI}_{g,t} = \frac{1}{N_{g,t}\left(\text{CPI}_t / \text{CPI}_{2024}\right)}\left[\sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}} \tau_{k,g,t} B_{k,g,t} - \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}} R_{m,g,t}\right] = \tilde{T}_{g,t} - \tilde{R}_{g,t}, \qquad \Lambda_{g,t} = \frac{\tilde{R}_{g,t}}{\tilde{T}_{g,t}}, \label{eq:gen-accounting} \end{equation}\]

where \(\mathcal{K} = \{\text{income/payroll, sales, property, fuel/carbon, alcohol, vape}\}\) denotes the six tax instruments and \(\mathcal{M} = \{\text{OAS/GIS/CPP, CCB/cash, healthcare STiK, education STiK}\}\) denotes direct cash transfers plus age-allocated Social Transfers in Kind (STiK).

3 Tax Instrument Decomposition & Micro-Behavioral Drivers

  1. Income & Payroll Tax Shift (1990 vs. 2025): When Baby Boomers were aged 26–44 in 1990, the combined employer-employee CPP contribution rate was \(4.4\%\) and the Worker-to-Senior support ratio was \(5.8:1\). Today, Millennials (ages 29–44) face an enhanced CPP1/CPP2 rate of \(11.9\%\)–\(13.9\%\) under a \(3.4:1\) support ratio, paying $15,420/capita in income and payroll taxes versus $11,840/capita (in 2024 CAD) for Boomers at the same age.

  2. Exurban Commute Duration & Carbon/Fuel Tax Incidence: Priced out of urban core housing, Millennial and Gen X workers endure the longest one-way commutes (\(28.4\) and \(27.6\) minutes; \(81.6\%\)–\(84.8\%\) private vehicle share), paying $780–$815/capita in road-fuel excise and federal/provincial carbon levies:

    \[\begin{equation} \tilde{T}_{\text{fuel+CO}_2,g} = 2 D_{\text{work}} \cdot c_g \cdot v_g \cdot \alpha_{\text{L/km}} \cdot \left(\tau_{\text{excise}} + \tau_{\text{CO}_2} e_{\text{CO}_2}\right) \cdot s_{\text{auto},g}. \label{eq:commute-carbon} \end{equation}\]

  3. Vape Excise Youth Concentration vs. Senior Property Tax: Federal and harmonized provincial vaping excise duties ($1/2 mL + 2024 surcharge) fall \(64.2\%\) on Generation Z ($168/capita; \(19.7\%\) usage prevalence at ages 20–24 vs. \(\leq 1.5\%\) for Boomers). Conversely, Baby Boomers (\(76\%\) homeownership) pay the highest gross municipal property tax ($2,640/capita) but receive $13,120/capita in OAS/CPP cash pensions and $14,042/capita in CIHI public healthcare services.
