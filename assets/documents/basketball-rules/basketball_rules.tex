\begin{abstract}
Seven familiar basketball challenges become a small sporting series: win a
challenge, choose the next one, and be first to four wins. This article preserves
the house mechanics and their joke names, separates optional generosity from
competitive scoring, and makes missing decisions visible rather than inventing
them. The rules are not official basketball regulations. Court diagrams are
original schematics, not measurements or instructions to attempt unsafe contact.
\end{abstract}
\tableofcontents

\section{The series: first to four, not a fixed playlist}
For the normal two-player format, the first player to win four challenges wins
the best-of-seven series. The winner of each challenge chooses the next challenge;
the game order is set during play. Stop when someone reaches four. There can be
four, five, six or seven played challenges, not seven compulsory games after a clinch.
The challenge menu is Shooting, HORSE, 1v1, Backboard, 3 Point, Rim and Bump.

The source log writes \textbf{L} for Liam and \textbf{O} for Owen, in the order of
the challenge winners listed. Thus a winner code describes who won each listed
game; it is not a list of who picked it. The first picker is unrecorded. All
observed series start with Shooting, but that observation does not prove a fixed
opening rule. Whether repeats are allowed is unspecified: agree the repeat
policy and first-picker procedure before play, rather than assuming either.

\subsection{Rule hierarchy and the pre-play agreement}
The numbered game clauses below preserve the supplied zero-based or one-based
starts. A \textbf{safety/consent override} always takes priority. An
\textbf{operational interpretation} is a practical reading requiring confirmation,
not a newly discovered owner rule. An \textbf{agree before play} note identifies a
gap in the supplied mechanics. Do not settle such a gap by retroactively choosing
the interpretation most favorable to the current leader.

Before starting, select exact court markers, an accessible shooting range,
participants, rest arrangements, the first picker, repeat policy, simultaneous
finish handling, and any game-specific gaps listed below. Use a clear court with
permission to play and film. Check the surface, balls and basket; no participant
needs to attempt a jump or distance outside their ability. Pause when conditions
or participation change. These preparations do not create a new scoring system.

\section{Safety and consent override: real rules above the jokes}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ball-to-ball only in a clear court.} Bump's interference allowance
is exclusively ball-to-ball. Do not target or hit any people, including players
who are eliminated, resting, or on their phone. No hands or body on an opponent's
ball. Pause for bystanders and any occupied return or rebound lane. If a tactic
cannot be separated safely from people, do not attempt it.
\item \textbf{Reckless contact stops play.} Stop the action, check the people
involved, provide appropriate first aid and obtain medical help when needed.
Resume only when it is safe and everyone agrees. No scoring dispute outranks
care. The old credit-card-for-24-hours/Amazon joke is not an enforceable rule:
there is no credit-card sharing, no passwords and no financial access.
\item \textbf{Dinner and costume bits are voluntary, mutually agreed opt-in.}
Nobody owes a purchase or a performance because of a joke. No coerced humiliation;
declining a tiara, lipstick, miniskirt, meal, filming or publication creates no
competitive penalty. Agreement can be withdrawn without ridicule.
\item \textbf{Trash talk needs consent and personal boundaries.} Stop when asked.
No hate, threats, unwanted personal attacks or encouragement of serious fouls.
If participants enjoy YOUR MOM, skill or dating jokes, that permission is local
and revocable, not a license to involve other people or expose private information.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mercy is an offer, not a debt}
Where the game allows unlimited mercy, offers have no fixed numeric cap. The
receiver uses the honor system and may reject a mercy if they feel they have
already lost. ``Out of mercies'' below means there are no more accepted or agreed
mercy attempts to play, not that an invented universal maximum has been reached.
Agree and record what each accepted mercy grants before using it.

Keep the dinner conventions distinct. In Shooting, the person receiving the
most mercies usually buys dinner for the loser or losers; this is not strict.
HORSE's version expects the most-mercy recipient to buy dinner for the others,
win or lose. Both are jokes subject to voluntary agreement, not a unified payment
obligation. Backboard and Rim inherit the Shooting-style mercy/dinner context.
The actual log does not record mercy counts or establish anyone's dinner debt.

\section{SHOOTING}
\begin{enumerate}[start=1]
\item Each player has \textbf{five attempts per shot location}. Begin near the
basket, then use the free-throw line, three-point line, near logo edge, logo
center, half court, far logo center, and so on. ``And so on'' is supplied, not a
complete measured list: agree the exact marks and any later locations on the court.
\item Advance to the next location when all players have made the current shot.
It is one successful shot at each location for each player, not five required makes.
\item Offer unlimited mercies for the vibes. The receiver should honorably
reject an offer if they feel they have lost. The person with the most mercies
usually buys dinner for the loser(s), but this is not strict and remains voluntary.
\item When both players fail at the same location, they may agree mutual
mercies in any amount, typically two. Two is a usual choice, not a mandatory cap
or an automatic extension awarded without agreement.
\item \textbf{HIGH JUMP RULES.} After both players are out of mercies, compare
the attempts taken at the previous successful shot: the player who made that
shot in the fewest attempts wins. This is called HIGH JUMP RULES because both
creators competed in high jump; the countback is their owner-supplied house rule.
If the previous-shot attempt counts tie, compare the shot before it in the same
way, and keep going back shot by shot as far as the very first shot. If the
counts are tied all the way back to the beginning, a \textbf{SUDDEN DEATH} starts
at the shot both players missed: the location with five, four or three attempts
in Shooting, Rim and Backboard respectively. It continues until a player makes
that shot. If it goes on too long, the players can mutually agree to move to a
closer shot. Read literally, a miss with no previous successful shot also goes
straight to SUDDEN DEATH; agree before play whether sudden-death attempts
alternate shot for shot.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What the scorekeeper needs}
Record attempts at every shared successful location, not merely the final miss.
Keep ordinary attempts and accepted mercies distinguishable, and agree whether
mercy attempts enter the countback total; that accounting detail is not supplied.
For example, if both fail later and A took one attempt at the previous successful
shot while B took three, HIGH JUMP RULES favors A. That is an illustration, not an entry
in the historical log. Equal counts send the countback to the shot before, then
the one before that, back to the first shot, so record attempts at every location.

\section{HORSE}
\begin{enumerate}[start=0]
\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\item Determine the order by sudden-death, shot-for-shot shooting at an agreed
distance. For more than two players, agree how that shootout establishes the full order.
\item Set doable trick shots, distances or rebounded jumpers. No weird
\textbf{RUIN THE VIBE} shot: do not require a dunk from someone who cannot dunk,
involve bystanders or require anything illegal. Announce material parameters
before the shot, including location, bounce/rebound requirements and release style.
\item If the setter misses, the next player gets to set. Continue passing the
set until somebody makes a shot.
\item The other players replicate the successful set's preannounced parameters.
Ambiguity leaves room within reason, not permission to be a \textbf{WEASILY WESLEY}.
Clarify disagreements rather than exploiting an unannounced technicality.
\item A missed replication adds one letter of HORSE to that player's tally.
\item Reaching E eliminates that player. The last player remaining wins.
\item The setter should be generous about mercy when a player faces their final
E. The player receiving the most mercies is expected, in the house joke, to buy
dinner for the others whether they win or lose. The \textbf{BAD VIBES BETTY}
tiara, purple lipstick and miniskirt-for-dinner bit is an optional costume joke,
not a punishment. Every meal and costume choice is voluntary, mutually agreed
opt-in; no coerced humiliation, and declining has no game penalty.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Letter states, not basketball points}
The states are no letters, H, HO, HOR, HORS and HORSE. A missed set changes who
sets; a missed replication changes the replicator's letter state. Those are not
the same event. Agree the setter rotation after everyone finishes replication,
the treatment of simultaneously final-E players and the number of attempts
granted by a mercy; the supplied rules do not fully specify these details.
Historical HORSE strings such as ``5-3'' remain raw, uninterpreted records here:
do not silently decide their letter orientation or reinterpret them as points.

\section{1v1}
\begin{enumerate}[start=0]
\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\item Begin with a sudden-death shootout to determine who starts. With extra
players, possible formats are alternating possessions for a predetermined
\textbf{EVEN} count, king of the hill with a new player after possession changes,
or a four-player tournament. Choose the structure and details before play.
\item Check the ball respectfully by passing it back and forth before play.
\item A basket from outside the three-point arc scores \textbf{two points};
inside the arc scores \textbf{one point}. Agree line calls before play.
\item The first player to reach or exceed \textbf{seven points} wins. More
players can agree a higher target. Win by two is not specified; do not add it.
A two-point shot can therefore finish a seven-point game at eight.
\item An offensive foul changes possession. An egregious foul, or repeated
less-severe fouls, leads to a one-point free throw under the supplied house rule.
Agree shooter and restart administration rather than importing a league rulebook.
\item A major defensive foul earns a free throw. For a minor defensive foul,
\textbf{IM NOT A BIG OL BABY} allows waiving the call, or an extra possession
after a turnover if the minor fouls are repeated. Do not use the joke to pressure
someone to accept contact or play through injury.
\item Consensual \textbf{YOUR MOM}, skill and dating trash talk is allowed
within personal boundaries. It is not encouragement of serious fouls. Stop when
asked; no hate, threats or unwanted involvement of others.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Possession details that must be agreed}
Made-basket possession is unspecified: do not silently assume winner's outs or
loser's outs. Clearing rules, the player who takes each free throw, free-throw
rebound handling and the restart after a free throw are also unspecified.
Confirm how the optional extra possession is tracked. All contact remains
subject to the safety override; reckless contact stops the game for care, rather
than merely being traded for points. For extra-player versions, set the even
possession count or bracket, substitutions, target and ties in advance.

\section{BACKBOARD}
\begin{enumerate}[start=0]
\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\item The setup must contact the \textbf{backboard}. Catch the ball before it
touches the ground while you are airborne, and release the shot before landing.
The idea is to simulate being covered immediately after a rebound; no defender
needs to make contact for this simulation.
\item Each player has \textbf{three attempts per location}, using the same
progression: near basket, free throw, three-point line, near logo edge, logo
center, half court, far logo center, and so on at agreed court markers.
\item Advance when all players make the shot at the current location.
\item Offer unlimited mercy for the vibes, with the receiver honorably free to
reject it if they feel they have lost. The most-mercy recipient usually buys
dinner for the loser(s) in the Shooting-style context; it is not strict and is
only a voluntary, mutually agreed opt-in.
\item If both players fail, agree mutual mercies in any amount, typically two.
They are not automatic and two is not a cap.
\item \textbf{HIGH JUMP RULES.} After both players are out of mercies, compare
the attempts taken at the previous successful shot: the player who made that
shot in the fewest attempts wins. This is called HIGH JUMP RULES because both
creators competed in high jump; the countback is their owner-supplied house rule.
If the previous-shot attempt counts tie, compare the shot before it in the same
way, and keep going back shot by shot as far as the very first shot. If the
counts are tied all the way back to the beginning, a \textbf{SUDDEN DEATH} starts
at the shot both players missed: the location with five, four or three attempts
in Shooting, Rim and Backboard respectively. It continues until a player makes
that shot. If it goes on too long, the players can mutually agree to move to a
closer shot. Read literally, a miss with no previous successful shot also goes
straight to SUDDEN DEATH; agree before play whether sudden-death attempts
alternate shot for shot.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Observe contact, catch and release separately}
A board touch does not by itself complete the shot. The observer must be able
to see the ball's setup contact, the airborne catch before ground contact, the
release before landing, and the final make. Agree disputed-attempt handling and
how mercy attempts enter the countback before play. A safe landing area takes
priority over preserving an attempt. Do not import Rim's BALL exception into
Backboard: that exception is supplied for Rim, not specified here.

\section{3 POINT}
\begin{enumerate}[start=1]
\item Make five three-point shots in sequence: the corner at
$0^\circ$, the $45^\circ$ wing, the $90^\circ$ top, the $135^\circ$ wing and the
$180^\circ$ corner, then make \textbf{HALF COURT}. Either corner may be the
starting corner; reverse the order when starting at the other end of the arc.
These angles name spots, not surveyed court coordinates. The supplied rule gives
no fixed attempt budget for the ordinary sequence.
\item Players may help return balls, but may not interfere with shots.
Assistance is not required; otherwise each player self-rebounds. Keep return
lanes clear and do not involve an unconsenting bystander as a rebounder.
\item Mercy lets the otherwise-losing player make their next shots back-to-back,
without a miss, all the way through half court. If they make the whole chain,
\textbf{``reset the game where it previously ended.''} That wording is preserved:
it does not uniquely identify which players' locations, progress or clock state
are restored. Decide the exact reset state before play. A miss does not earn
that reset; do not replace the house comeback with an invented bonus-point system.
\item Recording completion time is optional. The owner's impression is that
timing often makes it feel like fewer shots are needed; this is an impression,
not a demonstrated statistical effect. Rebounding help, attempt counts and losing
times were not standardized in the supplied history.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Finishing and timing: an explicit interpretation}
Treating the first finisher as the winner is an \textbf{operational interpretation}
of the race and otherwise-losing-player mercy, not an explicit standalone winner
clause in the source. Confirm it before play, including simultaneous finishes,
when a mercy may be offered, clock start/stop conditions and the exact reset
state. Missing timing is not zero seconds. A quicker recorded winner time does
not prove fewer attempts, better shooting skill or a fair comparison of sessions.

\section{BUMP}
\begin{enumerate}[start=0]
\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\item Use rock-paper-scissors to determine the first player, or who gets to pick
the first player; agree which interpretation applies before starting. Going
first is a disadvantage ``unless Liam'' is the owner's joke, not an objective
finding from the data.
\item Fix the starting shooting location with the least-skilled player in
mind. Agreed different locations may be used for a \textbf{SKILL ISSUE}; this
requires agreement rather than a unilateral handicap.
\item The first player shoots first. Obviously.
\item If the second player is tricked by a fakeout and shoots too early, they
must retrieve their ball and return to the start: \textbf{SILLY BOY YOU SHOULDVE
BEEN PAYING ATTENTION}. The consequence is lost time, not contact or humiliation.
\item If the second player makes before the first player, the first player is
eliminated.
\item After the initial shot, a rebound may be shot from anywhere, subject to
clear-court safety and no intrusion on bystanders.
\item If the first player makes before the second, they pass their ball to the
next queued player, who becomes the second player. With only two remaining,
the roles swap. Agree queue administration after elimination before play.
\item The legal interference allowance is \textbf{ball-to-ball only}: no hands
or body on an opponent's ball. In a completely clear court, examples are using
your held ball to collide with the other ball; throwing your ball to block the
other ball's flight, at which Owen is ``annoyingly good'' in the owner joke;
using a ball up through the net to reject a basket, while watching for fakeouts;
or redirecting a bad rebound using your ball. These are conditional examples,
not exemptions from safety. Do not target or hit any people, including those
eliminated, resting, or on their phone. Pause for bystanders, protect safe
landing/retrieval space and skip any tactic that cannot stay safely ball-to-ball.
\item Careless or reckless human contact stops play for a safety check and
appropriate first aid, with medical help when needed. The credit-card-for-24-hours
Amazon bit is a joke that is \textbf{not a real rule}. No credit cards, passwords
or financial access are requested, surrendered, shared or used as a penalty.
Only resume when the court is safe and the participants agree.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Two to six players and the multiplayer extension}
Two-player play uses the normal first-to-four finish. For three to six players,
the house extension considers the most wins after seven games if nobody has
already reached four. A maximum of six belongs in the playing group; for a
larger gathering, use brackets with two or three players per round. Agree how
challenge winners, advancement and the next picker work for the chosen structure.
This is an extension, not evidence that the two-player data engine supports it.

If players tie after seven, for example a $3$--$3$--$1$ split, the supplied house
rule uses games won by losing players to break the tie. \textbf{The selection
mechanism is unspecified.} Agree which eligible game is selected, which players
take part, who chooses it, how its outcome resolves the tie and what to do if it
does not. Do not claim the sentence gives a unique result or a complete exact
algorithm. The example is hypothetical, not an additional recorded series.
